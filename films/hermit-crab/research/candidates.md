## 1. The Shell

**Logline:** A shell narrates its own career as a house. Six tenants, every one of them temporary, every one of them eaten. Now it is empty again, and this time it intends to choose.

**Key images:** the shell alone on wet sand with the light coming from above; a queue of eyes arriving at its mouth; the shell being dragged across the pool by something inside it; a tenant's legs leaving and not coming back; the shell dry on a windowsill with a price sticker on it.

**Worlds and places:** one rock pool, seen from where the shell lies. The shell cannot move, so the world arrives at it: every shot is a new visitor entering the same frame.

**Form:** a first-person monologue by an object, in panels — each shot is one entry in the shell's history. 30 s, flat vector, thick ink outline.

**Seed:** s3 — form: a comic strip in panels · tone: noir.

**Why it is strong:** the voice is the film. An object that has buried six tenants, and has opinions about a seventh, is a narrator nobody expects, and it costs one hero shape instead of a cast.

## 2. The Duel

**Logline:** Two hermit crabs want the same shell, so they fight for it under the pool's rules: no blood, no retreat, and the loser walks away with nothing on its back. The winner gets the shell. Then the ring of eyes that watched the fight steps forward, and it turns out to have been a queue.

**Key images:** two crabs circling one shell, claws raised, in a ring of watching eyes; the shell between them, untouched, the prize and the referee; sand kicked into a cloud; the loser walking off naked and small; the ring of eyes taking one step in, all of them wanting the same shell.

*(Round 3 note: the "one size too small" try-on is dropped — it was prediction 1's key image and payoff.)*

**Worlds and places:** a flat arena of wet sand between two rocks, at crab level. The onlookers are a ring of eyes at the edge of frame, never explained.

**Form:** a duel, shot like a boxing match that nobody is commentating. 30 s, flat vector, one gag per exchange, the narrator calm and administrative throughout.

**Seed:** s1 — form: a cross-section · engine: a dare · tone: lyrical, played against a fight.

**Why it differs in kind:** it is the only one that is physical and competitive rather than tender or retrospective, and its turn is arithmetic — the prize comes with a queue, so winning is a debt of vigilance.

## 3. The Tenant

**Logline:** The crab finds the perfect shell with one small life still inside it. It cannot evict it, so it carries them both, and every kindness makes it slower.

**Key images:** a shell with two exits; a passenger's eye at the second opening; the crab eating twice as much; the crab walking with a shell that is visibly too heavy for it; an empty patch of sand where the crab used to be.

**Worlds and places:** the pool, the open sand between the rocks where being slow is visible to everyone.

**Form:** a fable, warm for twenty seconds and then not. 30 s, flat vector.

**Seed:** s1 — form: a cross-section · engine: a dare · tone: lyrical.

**Turn (no bird):** the passenger grows until it fills the shell, then walks out with it. The crab is left outside, alive and unhoused: it gave something a home and the home was taken.
