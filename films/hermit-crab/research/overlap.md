## Verdicts

**1. The Shell — CLEAR.**
The idea is new in the strongest way available: the house is the narrator and every crab is a visitor, so the film's subject is not a search but a vacancy. No prediction has an object speaking, a career of tenants, or a house that intends to choose; prediction 4 (the house hunt) is a crab shopping under a warm comic narrator, and the strip of it that could be recognised — the queue of arrivals at one shell, the shell as property — is here a queue of meals, with no listing, no sign, no pun and no transaction. The key images are its own and each does work: light from above on an empty shell, a tenant's legs leaving and not coming back, the shell dragged by something inside it. The turn is a choice, not a punchline, and no prediction ends on an object deciding.
Nearest call, on the record: the last shot, the shell dry on a windowsill with a **price sticker**, sits in prediction 4's market world (prediction 4 puts a "for rent" sign on a whelk shell). It is not marked, because the film's joke is that the shell is still waiting to be chosen, not that it is for sale, and the sticker is set dressing in one shot. If the main chat wants the file clean of any brush, drop the sticker and keep the sill: the shot reads the same without a price on it. Its other clearance is worth stating plainly — it does not repeat the turtle film, whose crabs and gulls are predators on a hatchling's run, where here nothing is running and the shell is the one telling it.

**2. The Duel — SHARES, with prediction 1 (the upgrade), on its turn.**
The idea is new: a fight with rules, shot as a bout nobody is commentating, its onlookers a ring of eyes never explained; no prediction contains a contest between two crabs, and prediction 2 (the chase) is a predator arriving from above, which this film does not have at all. The naked loser, the sand cloud, the shell left untouched between them as prize and referee — none of that is predicted.
What repeats is the **fit**: the winner tries the shell and it is one size too small, and that wrong-size try-on *is* prediction 1's key image ("one shell visibly too big, the crab measuring itself against it") and prediction 1's payoff, inverted into a debt. Inversion is still the same element — round 2 removed the gull for exactly this reason — and here it carries the whole ending, so it is marked rather than named. Everything else about the candidate survives; it is offered with that one element changed, below.

**3. The Tenant — CLEAR.**
The bird stays gone, and with it the only element round 1 marked. What is left is an idea no prediction holds: a shell with a resident too small to be a threat, a kindness paid for in speed, a fable warm for twenty seconds and then not. Prediction 4 is the only prediction in the same territory and it is a different joke — nobody here is shopping, renting, listing or evicting; the crab cannot evict, and that refusal is the film. Prediction 1 is the one it comes closest to by vocabulary ("the perfect shell"), but the shell fits perfectly and the problem is the passenger, so prediction 1's size-mismatch image is not used; the try-on gag belongs to candidate 2, not here. The ending — the passenger outgrows the shell and walks out with it, the crab alive and unhoused — is a dark-funny arithmetic the film arrives at itself, not prediction 1's perfect fit and not prediction 2's escape. Against the turtle film: a hatchling crossing a beach under gulls is a run, and this is a crab carrying a passenger until it cannot; no image is shared.

## The three together

Two notes for the main chat, neither an overlap with anything outside:

The register is opposite (cold anthology versus warm fable) and the endings reversed (the shell chooses versus the crab is left out), so neither is the other. If the user picks one, the other is not a film to re-offer later in the same project.
